# Fourteen EU label schemes fit one engine

Of the 29 schemes (EU-27, with Belgium split into Flanders, Wallonia and Brussels), **14 can go into Label Explorer now (KEEP), 11 can go in once one specific input is found or one design decision is made (UNCERTAIN), and 4 should be left out (DROP: Poland, Lithuania, Malta, Cyprus)**. The KEEP group has absolute, published kWh/m²·yr class limits. A single simplified monthly EN ISO 52016/52000-style engine can feed all of them, as long as it outputs several quantities rather than one: heating need, final energy per carrier, total, non-renewable and fossil primary energy, and CO2, each normalised to the right national floor area. The hard cases are not about the physics. They fall into four groups: countries whose class depends on a same-geometry reference building (Czechia, Italy, Greece, Portugal, Cyprus, Lithuania); countries whose official threshold table was never found in a primary source (Flanders, Luxembourg, Latvia, Romania, Bulgaria); Sweden, which changed its method and scale on 1 October 2026; and Poland, which still has no classes in force. Almost every scale in the table is temporary. The 2024 EPBD recast (Directive 2024/1275) requires a closed A–G scale with A = zero-emission building (ZEB). Nobody transposed it on time, and on 15 July 2026 the Commission opened infringement procedures against all 27 Member States ([EC INF/26/1380](https://ec.europa.eu/commission/presscorner/api/files/document/print/en/inf_26_1380/INF_26_1380_EN.pdf)). Every country's scale should therefore be stored as versioned configuration, not code. Expect most scales to be replaced between 2027 and 2030.

My verdicts differ from the regional research notes in three places. Czechia, Italy and Greece are all **UNCERTAIN** here, although the notes kept Greece and Italy as KEEP/UNCERTAIN. ## Fourteen schemes map a single number onto published bands

### Heating-need scales: Slovenia and Croatia are the cleanest fits

Two countries classify directly on space-heating need, the raw output of a monthly EN ISO 52016 balance. **Slovenia** (Pravilnik, Uradni list RS 4/2023, in force 1 Feb 2023) classes on Q'H,nd,an per conditioned area A_use: **A1 0–10, A2 >10–15, B1 >15–25, B2 >25–35, C >35–60, D >60–105, E >105–150, F >150–210, G >210 kWh/m²·a**. Delivered and total primary energy and CO2 appear on the certificate without classes ([Uradni list RS 4/2023](https://www.uradni-list.si/glasilo-uradni-list-rs/vsebina/2023-01-0094)). The method is stationary/monthly via PURES-3 and TSG-1-004:2022. Its primary energy factors sit in PURES-3 Annex 1, which was too large to extract, but the label does not need them ([PURES-3 Q&A 2024](https://www.gov.si/assets/ministrstva/MNVP/Dokumenti/Graditev/PURES-3_Odgovori_2024.pdf)). The climate source was not verified.

**Croatia** (Ordinance NN 88/2017, consolidated with NN 90/2020) classes residential buildings on Q''H,nd,ref per useful heated area A_K, calculated with **reference climate data rather than site data: Zagreb-Maksimir for continental Croatia and Split-Marjan for the littoral**. The limits are **A+ ≤15, A ≤25, B ≤50, C ≤100, D ≤150, E ≤200, F ≤250, G >250 kWh/m²·a**. A+…C come from the official text on [zakon.hr](https://www.zakon.hr/c/podzakonski-propis/45373/nn-88-2017-%281.9.2017.%29%2C-pravilnik-o-energetskom-pregledu-zgrade-i-energetskom-certificiranju). D–G are [S] ([montazneidrvenekuce.info](https://www.montazneidrvenekuce.info/energetika/sto-je-energetski-razred-zgrade/261)). Do not confuse this scale with the Bosnian FBiH scale, which uses the same terms. The certificate also shows an Eprim class. Family-house NZEB requirements are Eprim ≤45 (continental) and ≤35 (littoral) kWh/m²·a ([EPBD-CA Croatia 2020](https://cdn1.gopublic.dk/epbd/Media/638373602039208336/Implementation-of-the-EPBD-in-Croatia-2020.pdf)). A new Law on Energy Efficiency in Buildings reportedly took effect on 1 January 2026 [S] ([regent.hr](https://regent.hr/en/blog/green-building-and-nzeb-standard-what-are-nearly-zero-energy-houses-and-why-does-the-eu-want-them-by-2030)). Whether it rescales the label is unknown. PEFs were not found, but they are only needed for the secondary Eprim display.

### Germany classes on final energy, so factors barely matter

Germany's act was renamed the **Gebäudemodernisierungsgesetz (GModG)**, promulgated in BGBl. 2026 I Nr. 226 on 28 July 2026, but the **residential label is unchanged**. It is still A+…H on final energy per Gebäudenutzfläche A_N: **A+ ≤30, A ≤50, B ≤75, C ≤100, D ≤130, E ≤160, F ≤200, G ≤250, H >250 kWh/(m²·a)** ([buzer Anlage 10 GModG](https://www.buzer.de/Anlage_10_GModG.htm); [§ 86](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/geg/__86.html)). Consumer blogs claiming an A–G house scale from May 2026 are contradicted by the promulgated text. Only non-residential buildings get an A–G scale relative to a reference building, from 2027 [S] ([nexvyra](https://nexvyra.de/fakten/gmodg-anlage-10a-energieeffizienzklassen-nichtwohngebaeude)). The method is DIN V 18599:2018-09 (paywalled), moving to DIN/TS 18599:2025-10 from 2027 [S]. The single reference climate is Potsdam (DIN V 18599-10 Annex E), confirmed only by a search snippet ([§ 20](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/geg/__20.html); [DIN Media](https://www.dinmedia.de/de/vornorm/din-v-18599-10/142651484)). A_N comes from DIN V 18599-1 Eq. 30 ([§ 25](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/geg/__25.html)). The commonly used **A_N = 0.32·V_e** is [U], and it typically gives 1.2–1.35 × living area, so the app needs a volume input. Current non-renewable factors are **electricity 1.8, gas 1.1, wood 0.2, DH supplier-certified**, with CO2 factors of electricity 560 and gas 240 g/kWh ([Anlage 4](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/geg/anlage_4.html); [Anlage 9](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/geg/anlage_9.html)). From 2027, compliance switches to total PE, with electricity 1.5 and a DH default of 0.7 [S] ([nexvyra Anlage 4](https://nexvyra.de/fakten/gmodg-22-anlage-4-primaerenergiefaktoren)). Compliance is relative (Q_P ≤ 0.55 × reference building, [§ 15](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/geg/__15.html)), but the label is not.

### Austria is two-dimensional and site-climate based

**OIB-RL 6, edition September 2025**, replaces the A++…G/fGEE label with seven classes **A–G that are assessed on both HWB_Ref,SK (heating need) and EEB_SK (final energy including household electricity), per m² of heated gross floor area, at the site climate**. Class A is only possible with decarbonised supply (electricity, biogenic fuels, renewable DH or CHP DH) ([RIS Tirol LGBl 59/2026 Anlage 6](https://www.ris.bka.gv.at/Dokumente/LgblAuth/LGBLA_TI_20260715_59/Anlage_59_TBV2026_Anlage6_KM.pdf)). The upper limits are:

| Class | HWB_Ref,SK (kWh/m²a) | EEB_SK (kWh/m²a) |
|---|---|---|
| A (decarbonised only) | ≤40 | ≤90 |
| B | ≤60 | ≤110 |
| C | ≤75 | ≤125 |
| D | ≤85 | ≤140 |
| E | ≤100 | ≤155 |
| F | ≤120 | ≤175 |
| G | >120 | >175 |

The method is monthly and officially mapped to EN ISO 52000-1/52016-1, which makes it the closest relative of the target engine. The factors for 2026–2035 are total / non-renewable: **electricity 1.43/0.43 (74 g CO2/kWh), gas 1.10/1.10 (201 g), renewable DH 1.27/0.07, non-renewable DH 1.19/0.80, CHP DH 0.71/0.05** (same source). Setpoints are back at 20 °C / 12 °C heating limit, which lowers HWB by about 12% compared with 2019/2023 ([AEA/BMWET brochure](https://www.tirol.gv.at/fileadmin/themen/bauen-wohnen/baupolizei/20260219_Zentrale_%C3%84nderungen_der_OIB_Richtlinie_6_im_Jahr_2025.pdf)). Three caveats. First, how the HWB and EEB classes combine was not found in the text, though the OVI implies the EEB class is the headline ("C fossil") ([OVI](https://www.ovi.at/fileadmin/Der_Energieausweis_neu.pdf)). Second, site climate data sits in paid ÖNORM B 8110-5. Third, the Länder adopt RL 6 (2025) at different dates (Tyrol, Upper Austria and Vienna are confirmed), so older A++…G certificates will coexist for years.

### Ireland reproduces almost one-to-one

Ireland's DEAP v4.2.7 (June 2025) is itself a **monthly, single-zone EN 13790-based method with one Dublin Airport climate**, cooling per EN ISO 52016-1, and a free English manual of 200+ pages ([DEAP Manual 4.2.7](https://www.seai.ie/sites/default/files/publications/DEAP_Manual.pdf)). Regulations signed in April 2026 introduced a new scale for BERs published from **24 May 2026: A0 ≤42 (plus ZEB criteria), A ≤75, B ≤150, C ≤225, D ≤275, E ≤325, F ≤375, G >375 kWh/m²·yr** total primary energy per total floor area at internal dimensions. These limits were read from the certificate mock-up; the "≤" convention is inferred ([SEAI Advertising Guidelines, July 2026](https://www.seai.ie/sites/default/files/2026-07/BER-Combined-Advertising-Guidelines.pdf); [Passive House Plus](https://passivehouseplus.ie/news/government/new-ber-takes-effect-while-zeb-details-remain-unclear)). A0 also requires ≤5 kgCO2eq/m²·yr, no on-site fossil fuel, grid responsiveness, carbon-free supply and NZEB compliance ([Engineers Ireland](https://www.engineersireland.ie/Engineers-Journal/News/six-locks-on-one-door-why-almost-nothing-in-ireland-will-earn-an-a0)). The old 15-band A1…G scale stays valid on certificates issued before that date. DEAP Table 8 gives **mains gas 1.1 (0.203 kgCO2/kWh)**, heating oil 1.1 and generic DH 1.05–1.1. Electricity is published separately and updated every year: 2.08 in the 2017 derivation ([SEAI](https://www.seai.ie/sites/default/files/publications/DEAP-Elec-Factors-2017.pdf)), and a later 2.19 / 0.473 [S, date unclear] ([LinkedIn](https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/deap-updates-electricity-heat-pumps-shay-kavanagh)). The current 2026 value still has to be extracted.

### Netherlands: official table, but the fossil factors sit behind NEN

The Dutch label is the best-documented threshold set in the study. Omgevingsregeling Bijlage IX, in force from 6 October 2026, classes dwellings on **EP2, primary fossil energy per m² usable floor area Ag**: **A++++ ≤0; A+++ ≤50; A++ ≤75; A+ ≤105; A ≤160; B ≤190; C ≤250; D ≤290; E ≤335; F ≤380; G >380 kWh/m²·yr** ([wetten.overheid.nl Bijlage IX](https://wetten.overheid.nl/jci1.3:c:BWBR0045528&bijlage=IX)). New buildings with permits filed after 29 May 2026 can also carry an **"A0" mark at EP2 ≤27** for single-family houses, which is 10% below BENG 2 = 30, provided they meet BENG 1/3 and have no on-site fossil emissions ([Staatscourant 2026, 18123](https://zoek.officielebekendmakingen.nl/stcrt-2026-18123.pdf)). The method is NTA 8800 2025+C1:2026, a CEN EPB implementation. BENG 1 depends on shape: ≤55 for Als/Ag ≤1.5, rising linearly above that ([RVO BENG 2019](https://www.rvo.nl/sites/default/files/2019/06/De%20BENG%20eisen%20juni%202019.pdf)). The commonly cited fossil factors (electricity 1.45, gas 1.0) are [U]; they live in paywalled NTA 8800 tables. De Bilt as the climate is [U]. NL used the Art. 19(2) derogation: the current scale stays until **1 January 2030**, when an A–G scale on **total** primary energy with a new method takes over ([IPLO](https://iplo.nl/thema/energiebesparing/ontwikkeling-wetgeving-energiebesparing/ontwikkelingen-europese-richtlijn-epbd-iv/toelichting-per-artikel-epbd-iv/)).

### Wallonia and Brussels: absolute scales, certifier-sourced

**Wallonia** classes on Espec, total primary energy per m² of heated floor area: **A++ <0, A+ 0–45, A 45–85, B 85–170, C 170–255, D 255–340, E 340–425, F 425–510, G >510 kWh/m²·yr** [S] ([Certinergie](https://www.certinergie.be/en/energy-performance-certificate/epc-certificate-wallonia/)). The Q-ZEN new-build rule (Ew ≤45, Espec ≤85) is official ([SPW Q-ZEN 2026](https://energie.wallonie.be/files/PEB/B%c3%a2timents%20r%c3%a9sidentiels/Nouvelle%20construction/Outils/Conseils%20pratiques%20-%20publications/Guide%20pratique%20pour%20construire%20votre%20logement%20QZen/brochure%20QZen_2026.pdf)). **Brussels** uses **A ≤45, B 46–95, C 96–150, D 151–210, E 211–275, F 276–345, G >345 kWh EP/m²·yr**, with +/–/0 sub-levels in A–E [S] (from a real certificate, [notary-hosted PEB certificate](https://immo.notaire.be/immoplatform-public-service_v1/api/attachments/356739/289057/730423.pdf)). That is consistent with the IEA 2011 entry, which splits A into A+ 0–15, A 16–30 and A− 31–45 ([IEA](https://www.iea.org/policies/2493)). New builds must reach CEP ≤45 and net heating need ≤15 ([Bruxelles Environnement](https://environnement.brussels/citoyen/reglementation/obligations-et-autorisations/exigences-et-procedure-de-la-reglementation-travaux-peb)). Neither region's PEFs were confirmed. Wallonia reportedly moved away from 2.5 for electricity [U]. Uccle as the climate is [U]. Both already use A–G letters, but whether A will be reserved for ZEBs is open ([Test-Aankoop](https://www.test-aankoop.be/woning-energie/isolatie/nieuws/epc-label-a-emissievrije-gebouwen-2030)).

### Slovakia and Hungary: absolute PE scales with a verification debt

**Slovakia** (vyhláška 364/2012 as amended by 35/2020) classes single-family houses on primary energy: **A0 ≤54, A1 55–108, B 109–216, C 217–324, D 325–432, E 433–540, F 541–648, G >648 kWh/m²·a**, plus A0+ for an nZEB that exports or stores energy. The values come from a text extraction of the annex [U, verify against the PDF] ([slov-lex 35/2020](https://static.slov-lex.sk/static/SK/ZZ/2020/35/20200310.html); [annex PDF](https://static.slov-lex.sk/pdf/prilohy/SK/ZZ/2020/35/20200310_5228103-2.pdf)). No PEFs or climate data were found. **Hungary's** brief assumed the old AA++…JJ scale, but that scale is obsolete. Since 1 November 2023 (9/2023 ÉKM), the scale has been **A+++…I as a percentage of the requirement**, with breakpoints at 0, 50, 90, 100, 130, 160, 200, 250, 310, 390 and 500% ([energiamernok.hu](https://energiamernok.hu/energetikai-besorolas-tablazat/)). For residential buildings the requirement is a fixed **76 kWh/m²·a nren PE excluding lighting**. That gives A++ ≤38, A+ ≤68, A ≤76, B ≤98.8, C ≤122, D ≤152, E ≤190, F ≤236, G ≤296, H ≤380, I >380 [S, snippet of the peer-reviewed Csoknyai article] ([MNB/Csoknyai](https://zoldpenzugyek.mnb.hu/sw/static/file/20241210_csoknayi_jogszabalyi_valtozasok_kovetkezmenyei_lakoingatlanok_besorolasara.pdf)). PEFs are official: **electricity nren 2.3 (tot 2.6, 455 g), gas 1.1 (297 g), DH 1.38** ([njt.jog.gov.hu 9/2023](https://njt.jog.gov.hu/jogszabaly/2023-9-20-8X)). Whether 76 is flat or formula-based for small houses is unconfirmed.

### Spain and the three Nordic/Baltic area-dependent scales

**Spain** builds its scale from stock statistics, but publishes it as absolute upper limits per climate zone and dwelling type. That makes it a lookup, not a reference run. There are **two parallel A–G ratings: one on EPnren (kWh/m²·yr) and one on CO2 (kgCO2e/m²·yr)**, per useful floor area, covering heating, cooling and DHW (no lighting in dwellings). Single-family, peninsula, zone D3: **A <54.2, B <87.8, C <136.1, D <209.3, E <375.6, F <473.2, G ≥473.2**; CO2 A 12.2 … F 100.4. Zones C2, E1 and A3 have their own tables ([MITECO/MITMA Calificación, Annex IV](https://www.eic.cat/promocio/e_serveis/normativa/doc_reconocidos_certificado_eficiencia_energetica/Calificaci%C3%B3n%20de%20la%20eficiencia%20energ%C3%A9tica%20de%20los%20edificios.pdf)). The downloaded copy may be a pre-publication version, because the cover date is "XX". The underlying formula C1 = (R·Io/Ir − 1)/(2(R − 1)) + 0.6 is also published. Spanish PEFs (electricity 1.954 nren/2.368 tot, gas 1.190) are **[U]** until checked against the 2016 Documento Reconocido.

## Eleven schemes need one missing piece first

**Moving targets (France, Sweden).** **France's** DPE thresholds are official, absolute and easy to apply. For homes over 40 m², the class is the worse of **CEP (kWhEP/m²) and EGES (kgCO2eq/m²): A <70/6, B <110/11, C <180/30, D <250/50, E <330/70, F <420/100, G at or above F**. What blocks France is everything else. The electricity factor is **1.9 for 2026 and 1.7 for DPEs from 1 January 2027** (arrêté of 19 August 2026) [S] ([Selectra](https://selectra.info/energie/actualites/renovation-energetique/dpe-2027-coefficient-electricite-passoires-sans-travaux)). **Poland** has no energy classes in force. The certificate shows an EP slider against the requirement. Its publication was never confirmed, and the thresholds circulating on blogs contradict each other (G >330, G >150 or G >400) ([GLOBEnergia](https://globenergia.pl/klasy-energetyczne-budynkow-juz-w-czerwcu-2026-roku/); [certwell.pl](https://certwell.pl/nowy-wzor-swiadectwa-2026/)). Poland's electricity factor of 3.0 dates from 2015 ([Balaras et al. 2023](https://www.mdpi.com/1996-1073/16/1/93)). Revisit Poland once the regulation appears in the Dziennik Ustaw. **Lithuania** (STR 2.01.02:2016) assigns A++…G from C1/C2 ratios of non-renewable PE to a national reference, combined with envelope U-value, airtightness, heat-recovery and renewable-share conditions. One kWh/m² value cannot reproduce that, and the consolidated text blocked automated access ([CA-EPBD LT 2018](https://cdn1.gopublic.dk/epbd/Media/638373598450195807/CA-EPBD-IV-Lithuania-2018.pdf)). **Malta's** dwelling EPC "does not define the energy performance in terms of a band" ([University of Malta study](https://www.um.edu.mt/library/oar//bitstream/123456789/19962/1/An%20investigation%20into%20the%20practical%20application%20of.pdf)). No numeric bands were found, and sources disagree on whether the scale runs A–G or A–H.

## The 2024 recast will rewrite most scales by 2030

Article 19 of Directive 2024/1275 requires a **closed A–G scale, with A = ZEB and G anchored to the worst performers of each national stock**. It allows an optional A+ for buildings at least 20% below the ZEB threshold that are net renewable producers. Countries already labelling ZEBs "A0" on 29 May 2026 may keep that label. The headline indicator is primary energy in kWh/(m²·yr), and the Annex V template, which also requires final energy, on-site renewable share and operational GHG, applies from 29 May 2026 ([EUR-Lex 2024/1275](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=OJ:L_202401275)). The "15% worst" figure for G appears in Annex II and the Commission proposal, not in Article 19 itself ([JRC135473](https://publications.jrc.ec.europa.eu/repository/bitstream/JRC135473/JRC135473_01.pdf)). Member States that rescaled between 2019 and May 2024 may wait until **31 December 2029** ([Test-Aankoop](https://www.test-aankoop.be/woning-energie/isolatie/nieuws/epc-label-a-emissievrije-gebouwen-2030)). No Member State met the deadline. The Commission sent letters of formal notice to all 27 on 15 July 2026, and whether reasoned opinions followed after September is unknown ([EC INF/26/1380](https://ec.europa.eu/commission/presscorner/api/files/document/print/en/inf_26_1380/INF_26_1380_EN.pdf); [EBC](https://www.ebc-construction.eu/news/energy-efficiency-the-commission-opens-epbd-infringement-procedure-against-all-member-states/)).

At country level, the picture as of November 2026 is mixed. **Ireland** (A0–G live since 24 May 2026) is the only clean, completed switch for dwellings. **Austria** pre-empted the recast with RL 6 (2025): A–G, A only with decarbonised supply, and ZEB levels from 2030. **Sweden** added A0 and then rebuilt its method in October 2026. **Netherlands** added an A0 mark but keeps A++++…G until 2030. **France** keeps A–G and adds a non-class "A0" mention from 2027 ([MySweetImmo](https://www.mysweetimmo.com/2026/08/18/diagnostic-immobilier-ce-que-le-dpe-europeen-dira-en-plus-au-1er-janvier-2027/)). **Germany** transposed late, kept A+…H for houses, and faces criticism from the DUH over EU-law conformity ([DUH](https://www.duh.de/fileadmin/user_upload/download/Projektinformation/Energieeffizienz/Gebaeude/Hintergrundbriefing_DUH_EPBD-Umsetzung_im_GModG.pdf)). **Estonia** re-tightened its tables in July 2026 without a ZEB class. **Finland** has a draft amendment. **Greece, Romania, Bulgaria, Hungary and Poland** are flagged by BPIE/EPBD.wise as still needing rescaling ([EPBD.wise D5.3](https://www.bpie.eu/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/Energy-Performance-Certificates-v5-Compressed.pdf)). No status was found for Denmark, Latvia, Lithuania, Czechia, Slovakia, Slovenia, Spain, Portugal, Italy, Malta, Cyprus, Croatia, Luxembourg or the Belgian regions. Because A is tied to each country's own ZEB definition and G to its own stock, **the recast harmonises the letters, not the kWh limits**. A "B" will still mean different things in different countries after 2030, so the app's purpose survives the recast.

## Implementation implications for the shared engine

**Outputs the engine must produce.** One heating-need number is not enough. Each run should return, monthly and annually: space heating and cooling need (SI, HR, AT HWB, LV, ES complementary); DHW need; auxiliary and fan electricity; final (delivered) energy per carrier (DE label, CZ delivered-energy class, AT EEB, PL draft ED); and three primary-energy variants. Those variants are **total PE** (IE, BE regions, RO, AT PEB, NL from 2030, FR CEP), **non-renewable PE** (CZ, HU, IT, ES, LV, LT, PL) and **fossil-only PE** (NL EP2, which subtracts on-site generation), plus **national "weighted energy"** for DK, FI, EE and SE, which is the same mechanism with different factor tables. It must also return **operational CO2** for France's double threshold, Spain's second rating, the Irish A0 test (≤5 kg) and AT/RO display. Austria additionally needs **household electricity** inside EEB. Results must be normalisable to at least these floor-area definitions: NL Ag, DE A_N (from heated volume), AT gross floor area BGF (net ≈ 0.80 × BGF), IE TFA (internal), DK heated gross area, FI A_netto, SE Atemp, FR S_REF, and the "useful/usable" areas of ES, IT, HU, HR and RO. The cleanest design computes absolute kWh once and converts per country from a single geometry input that includes heated volume.

**Carrier and renewable rules, not just factors.** Several labels depend on Boolean or accounting rules as well as factors: Austria's decarbonised/fossil flag; the "no on-site fossil" A0/ZEB tests in IE, SE, NL and FR; PV credited only for self-consumption in FI and LU; NL EP2 netting on-site generation. Each country config therefore needs a carrier-type flag and a PV-crediting rule alongside its factor table.

**Countries needing a second reference-building run.** CZ, IT, GR and PT need one run on the same geometry with national reference U-values and systems, followed by a ratio lookup. Cyprus and Lithuania also would, which is part of why they are dropped. Sweden needs only a tabulated requirement value per house type and size, not a run. Bulgaria's need depends on whether Rr,ref and Rs,ref are tabulated. A reference run is optional for compliance displays in DE (0.55 × reference), IE (EPC 0.30/CPC 0.35), LU and the Belgian E-peil/Ew, but none of their labels needs it.

**Countries needing climate zones or site climate.** A single climate per country suffices for DE, IE, DK, FI, NL and BE, and for SE once its Fgeo division is applied. Multiple climates are needed for FR (8 zones plus an altitude flag), ES (~40 zones), IT (6), PT (3 × 3), GR (4), HR (2 fixed stations) and RO (5). **Austria uses the actual site climate for the label**, so it needs a location-based climate model.

**Floor-area-dependent scales.** DK (constant + k/A), FI (linear in A_netto, three bands), EE (three area bands), LV (three area bands) and FR (homes ≤40 m²) must receive the reference area as an input to the class lookup. Possibly LU does too. The NL BENG 1 (Als/Ag) and AT ℓc formulas affect compliance and the AT ZEB A/B split, not the label bands.

**Store every scale as versioned configuration.** Known change dates already include NL 2030 (A–G on total PE), SE 1 Oct 2026, FR 1 Jan 2027 (electricity 1.7), DE 1 Jan 2027 (factors and DIN/TS 18599), AT edition changes by Land, IE 24 May 2026 and EE 9 July 2026. Pending changes include Flanders, Poland, Finland, Greece and every country still due to rescale by 2029. Each country entry should carry: scale version and validity dates, indicator, area definition, factor table with source and year, climate set, and a per-value source-quality flag (primary / secondary / unverified) that the app can show to users.

## The label gap is real, but its size is not yet nailed down

The idea behind the app, one building and many national labels, has a direct peer-reviewed precedent. **Jenkins, Sayfikar, Gomez and Fueyo (2024, Buildings 14(9):2906)** ran sample buildings through several national EPC methods. They argue that similar climates often give similar-looking ratings that "hide real differences" in method ([Heriot-Watt portal](https://researchportal.hw.ac.uk/en/publications/a-comparative-study-of-energy-performance-certificates-across-eur/)). A search summary of the paper reports that **about 38% of buildings got the same class across methods, 45% differed by one class, and one Slovenian school was C under the Croatian method and G under the Slovenian one**, mainly because of how gains and ventilation losses are treated ([MDPI](https://www.mdpi.com/2075-5309/14/9/2906)). These figures were **not verified in the full text** (the page returned 403), and which countries and how many buildings were compared is unconfirmed. Quote them in the app only as "reported", until checked. The Slovenian–Croatian case is a useful warning: both countries class on heating need, so even the "easy" schemes diverge through method conventions, not just thresholds. That matters for interpreting the app's output.

The **JRC 2023 survey** (Ruggieri, Maduta, Melica, JRC135473) is the best citable EU-27 baseline, though it is pre-recast. It found that **the upper limit of class A ranges from 0 to almost 300 kWh/(m²·yr) across countries, and the lower limit of G from 200 to 1005**. It also found that end uses differ (SI and DK reportedly count only space heating; only 18 countries include lighting), and that under reference-building methods "identical consumption can get different classes" because of size or climate ([JRC135473](https://publications.jrc.ec.europa.eu/repository/bitstream/JRC135473/JRC135473_01.pdf)). Note that the JRC description of DK conflicts with the BR18 text, which includes ventilation, cooling and DHW in the energy frame. Follow-up work by Sayfikar and Jenkins (2025) extends the comparison to new indicators ([SAGE](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/01436244241282076)). A separate strand compares certified with measured performance ([Energies 2025](https://doi.org/10.3390/en18226015)), which is a different gap from the cross-country one the app shows. No validation of a simplified EN ISO 52016 monthly engine against DIN V 18599, ÖNORM B 8110-6, 3CL or DEAP was found, so the app's own error versus official tools is unquantified.

## Conclusion

The research changes the framing of the app. Label Explorer is less a heat-balance problem than a **post-processing problem**. The monthly physics is shared and well understood, but the labels diverge on four things: which energy quantity is counted (need, final, total, non-renewable or fossil primary, CO2), which floor area divides it, which climate it is computed in, and whether the scale is absolute or tied to a reference building. A shared engine that exposes all of those intermediate quantities can serve 14 schemes today and roughly 22 once the missing tables are retrieved. Building the reference-building run once would unlock Czechia, Italy, Greece and Portugal together. Separately, the user will notice that the clean absolute scales cluster in northern and western Europe, while relative scales cluster in the south and east. That pattern will itself appear in any cross-country comparison the app produces.

The second lesson is about timing. With every Member State under infringement and most scales due to change by 2030, any snapshot the app shows is a 2026 picture. Its value lies in making the method differences visible, not in fixing letter grades. Versioned configuration, a per-value source-quality flag shown in the interface, and a clear "indicative, not a legal certificate" label are what make the app defensible for an energy-advice audience.
