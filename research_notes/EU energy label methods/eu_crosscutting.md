# EU-wide cross-cutting layer: EPBD 2024 recast and cross-country EPC comparisons

Research date: 10 November 2026. Every claim has a source URL. Where something is pre-2024, the year is flagged. Confidence notes are given where a source is secondary or was only seen as a search snippet.

## Q1. What does the 2024 EPBD recast mandate for EPC scales, by when, and which member states have already moved to the harmonised A–G scale?

### Takeaway
Directive (EU) 2024/1275, Article 19, requires a closed A–G scale. Class A is a zero-emission building, and class G holds the worst performers of the national stock when the scale is introduced; the "15%" figure for G is in Annex II, not in Article 19. EPCs must follow the Annex V template by 29 May 2026, which is also the general transposition deadline (Art. 35). No member state notified full transposition on time. On 15 July 2026 the Commission opened infringement procedures against all 27. I found no reliable per-country list of who has already switched to the new scale.

### Cited Findings
- Art. 19: the EPC shows the energy performance class "on a closed scale using only letters from A to G". Class A = zero-emission building (ZEB). Class G = the worst-performing buildings in the national stock at the time the scale is introduced. Member States must ensure "an appropriate distribution of energy performance indicators among the energy performance classes". — [EUR-Lex, Directive (EU) 2024/1275](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=OJ:L_202401275)
- The 15% worst-performing-stock figure sits in Annex II (national building renovation plan / G definition context), not in the Art. 19 text itself. The JRC (2023) quotes the Commission proposal as "The letter G shall correspond to the 15% worst-performing buildings in the national building stock at the time of the introduction of the scale". — [EUR-Lex 2024/1275](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=OJ:L_202401275); [JRC135473, 2023](https://publications.jrc.ec.europa.eu/repository/bitstream/JRC135473/JRC135473_01.pdf)
- Optional class A+: Member States may define A+ for buildings whose energy demand threshold is at least 20% below the ZEB threshold and which produce more on-site renewable energy per year than their total primary energy demand. — [EUR-Lex 2024/1275](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=OJ:L_202401275)
- A0 option: Member States that already label ZEBs as "A0" on 29 May 2026 may keep doing so. — [EUR-Lex 2024/1275](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=OJ:L_202401275)
- Main indicator: a "numeric indicator of primary energy use in kWh/(m2.y)", together with reference values such as minimum requirements. Validity "shall not exceed 10 years". Recommendations are required unless the building is already class A. — [EUR-Lex 2024/1275](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=OJ:L_202401275)
- Template deadline: "By 29 May 2026, the energy performance certificate shall comply with the template in Annex V." — [EUR-Lex 2024/1275](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=OJ:L_202401275)
- Annex V mandatory front-page elements include at least:
  - (a) the class
  - (b) annual primary energy use in kWh/(m2.y)
  - (c) annual final energy use in kWh/(m2.y)
  - (d) on-site renewable energy as a % of energy use
  - (e) operational GHG emissions

  Further items exist but were not read in this session. — [EUR-Lex 2024/1275](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=OJ:L_202401275)
- Art. 35 transposition deadline is 29 May 2026 for Arts 1–3, 5–29 and 32 and Annexes I–III and V–X. Art. 17(15) (fossil-boiler incentives) had an earlier deadline of 1 January 2025. — [EUR-Lex 2024/1275](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=OJ:L_202401275)
- Commission guidance: Commission Notice C/2025/6438 (OJ C, 18 Dec 2025), "providing guidance on new or substantially modified provisions of the recast EPBD". Its Annex 3 covers EPCs (Arts 19–21, Annex V). The Annex 3 body text was not retrieved in this session. — [EUR-Lex C/2025/6438](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/C/2025/6438/oj/eng); a PDF copy is at [afec.es](https://www.afec.es/documentos/english/communication-2025-6438.pdf)
- Transposition status (secondary source, dated 23 July 2026):
  - The Commission statement of 15 July 2026 launched infringement procedures against all 27 MS for not fully transposing 2024/1275.
  - "None met the general transposition deadline of 29 May 2026".
  - MS had two months to notify measures, after which reasoned opinions may follow.
  - Related actions: in March 2026, 19 MS were targeted over late National Building Renovation Plans; in November 2025, Estonia, Italy and Hungary were targeted over the boiler-incentive ban.

  — [EBC Construction news](https://www.ebc-construction.eu/news/energy-efficiency-the-commission-opens-epbd-infringement-procedure-against-all-member-states/)
- EPBD.wise (May 2026) recommends that MS "implement/maintain EPC rescaling in full alignment with the recast EPBD, ensuring a harmonised A-G scale where class A corresponds to ZEB standard, class G captures the worst-performing share". It also recommends pairing rescaling with communication campaigns. Three of its four focus countries (BG, GR, HU, PL) explicitly call for changes to EPC design, layout and rescaling, so rescaling was not finished there as of May 2026. — [EPBD.wise D5.3, Cleto & Marques (ADENE), May 2026 (BPIE-hosted)](https://www.bpie.eu/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/Energy-Performance-Certificates-v5-Compressed.pdf); summary also on [BUILD UP](https://build-up.ec.europa.eu/system/files/2026-07/26-2026-9_Development%20of%20energy%20performance%20certificates%20Policy%20guideline%20summary.pdf)
- Ireland: a secondary source says the current A1…G scale (15 bands) will be replaced by an A–G scale under transposition. A claim that this happened on 24 May 2026 was found in only one search snippet and could not be corroborated (low confidence). — search result referencing [Climate Council Ireland EPBD factsheet](https://www.climatecouncil.ie/councilpublications/secretariatfactsheets/FS5%20EPBD.pdf)

### Inferences
- For the app, the safe statement is: "As of late 2026 no Member State had notified complete transposition by the deadline, and infringement procedures are open against all 27." Each country's rescaling status must come from the per-country research, not from an EU-level tracker.
- Because G is anchored to each national stock and A to each national ZEB definition, the recast harmonises the letters and the template, not the kWh thresholds. A "B" will still mean different kWh/(m2.y) in different countries, which is the core point of the label-gap app.

### Gaps
- No official Commission per-country table of transposition or rescaling status was found. The EUR-Lex "National transposition measures" page for 32024L1275 would be the primary check and was not retrieved.
- The text of Annex 3 of C/2025/6438 on EPC rescaling guidance was not read (the document is about 750k characters).
- Whether reasoned opinions were issued after September 2026 is unknown.

## Q2. Which comparative sources give per-country EPC class thresholds and indicators, and how recent are they?

### Takeaway
The most complete and recent official EU-27 compilation is the JRC Technical Report "Progress on the implementation of Energy Performance Certificates in EU" (Ruggieri, Maduta, Melica, 2023, JRC135473). It has per-country chapters with class boundaries, indicators, end-uses and floor area, all pre-recast. Newer (2024–2026) work focuses on qualitative and policy comparison rather than threshold tables.

### Cited Findings
- JRC 2023 (EUR 31730 EN, doi:10.2760/522233), based on a survey of MS. Its Chapter 5 gives per-country rules, class boundaries and specimen EPCs for all 27 (Belgium split into Brussels, Flanders and Wallonia). — [JRC135473 PDF](https://publications.jrc.ec.europa.eu/repository/bitstream/JRC135473/JRC135473_01.pdf); [OP publication page](https://op.europa.eu/en/publication-detail/-/publication/acacea4b-9fbe-11ee-b164-01aa75ed71a1)
- Key cross-country facts from JRC 2023 (pre-recast):
  - Number of classes and labels:
    - Only Poland and Malta had no energy classes. Poland planned to introduce them in 2023.
    - Most MS use 7–9 classes. Exceptions: Latvia 6, Italy 10, Hungary 12, Netherlands 12 (11 for residential), Ireland 15.
  - How boundaries are set:
    - Fixed kWh boundaries in BE-Brussels, BE-Flanders, BE-Wallonia, BG, DK, DE, EE, IE (residential only), FR, HR, LV, LU, NL, AT, RO, SI, SK, FI. In DK and FI the boundaries also depend on floor area.
    - Reference-building (ratio) method in CZ, IE (non-residential), GR, ES, IT, CY, LT, HU, PT, SE.
    - The upper limit of class A ranges from 0 to almost 300 kWh/(m2·yr). The lower limit of class G ranges from 200 to 1005 kWh/(m2·yr).
    - Wallonia and Germany define classes only for residential buildings.
  - End-uses included:
    - SI and DK count only space heating, without auxiliaries and DHW (as reported by JRC).
    - LU (residential), DE and AT exclude ventilation and air conditioning. IE excludes AC for residential.
    - Only 18 countries include lighting, 5 of them only for non-residential.
    - Most use net heated floor area.

  — [JRC135473](https://publications.jrc.ec.europa.eu/repository/bitstream/JRC135473/JRC135473_01.pdf)
- JRC 2023 recommendation: the main indicator should be calculated primary energy. It lists open issues: fixed versus reference-building boundaries, and old and new EPCs co-existing on the market during their 10-year validity. — [JRC135473](https://publications.jrc.ec.europa.eu/repository/bitstream/JRC135473/JRC135473_01.pdf)
- BPIE 2014, "Energy Performance Certificates across the EU – A mapping of national approaches" (2014, outdated). — [BPIE PDF](https://bpie.eu/wp-content/uploads/2015/10/Energy-Performance-Certificates-EPC-across-the-EU.-A-mapping-of-national-approaches-2014.pdf)
- EPBD Concerted Action (CA-EPBD) CT5 "Certification and Training – Status in 2022" (with annex). EPBD.wise used it as the cross-country reference for management, database, rescaling, QC and training. — [CA-EPBD CT5 2022 PDF](https://cdn1.gopublic.dk/epbd/Media/638373594077934858/CT5-Certification-and-Training-Status-in-2022-with-annex.pdf)
- QualDeEPC D2.1 "Report on local EPC situation and cross-country comparison matrix" (Gokarakonda, Venjakob, Thomas, 2020). — [QualDeEPC D2.1](https://qualdeepc.eu/wp-content/uploads/2020/04/QualDeEPC_D2.1_Final_V2.pdf)
- crossCERT D3.4 "Cross-country comparison of format and nature of recommended improvements in different EPCs" (2024). — [crossCERT D3.4](https://www.crosscert.eu/fileadmin/user_upload/crossCert_D3.4_Cross-country_comparison_recommended_improvements_EPCs.pdf)
- NextGen EPC Cluster (crossCERT, X-tendo, QualDeEPC, D^2EPC, EPC RECAST, U-CERT, ePANACEA, EDYCE and others) recommendations for the recast EPBD (2026). — [crossCERT newsletter](https://www.crosscert.eu/news/newsletters/the-latest-newsletter/nextgen-epc-clusters-recommendations-for-the-recast-energy-performance-of-buildings-directive) (cited in [EPBD.wise D5.3](https://www.bpie.eu/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/Energy-Performance-Certificates-v5-Compressed.pdf))
- EPBD.wise "Energy Performance Certificates: Policy needs and best practices" (2024). — [BUILD UP PDF](https://build-up.ec.europa.eu/system/files/2024-08/EPBD.wise_EPCs%20policy%20needs%20and%20best%20practices.pdf)
- "EPC thresholds by country": a one-page chart of kWh/m2/yr thresholds for HR, UK, ES, DK, DE, CZ, FR, NO, Brussels, FI, SE, PT, IT, IE, AT, Wallonia, NL, BG, Flanders, RO and SK. It was produced 27 Feb 2024 by European DataWarehouse. The PDF layout is not machine-readable, there is no methods note, and it should be treated as a secondary visual only. — [eurodw.eu PDF](https://eurodw.eu/wp-content/uploads/EPC-thresholds-by-country.pdf)
- Sayfikar & Jenkins (2025), "Energy performance certificate calculation methodologies across Europe and accommodating new performance indicators", Building Services Engineering Research & Technology. — [SAGE](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/01436244241282076)
- OID/ESREI (June 2022), "Standardising European EPCs – a crucial step for the energy transition". It is a real-estate-industry comparison. — [GlobalABC PDF](https://globalabc.org/sites/default/files/2022-07/Standardising%20European%20EPCs%20-%20A%20Crucial%20Step%20for%20the%20Energy%20Transition%20in%20the%20Building%20Sector.pdf)

### Inferences
- For threshold citations in the app, the JRC 2023 report is the best single EU-wide citable source. It should be labelled "pre-recast, 2023" and checked against each national rescaled scale.
- The DK/FI floor-area dependence and the reference-building countries (CZ, GR, ES, IT, CY, LT, HU, PT, SE, plus IE non-residential) cannot be expressed as a single kWh threshold table. The app needs per-country logic for these.

### Gaps
- No post-2026 (post-rescaling) EU-27 threshold compilation was found.
- The EPBD CA 2022 per-country implementation reports and the EU Building Stock Observatory EPC data were not retrieved in this session.
- The X-tendo and D^2EPC deliverables were not individually checked.

## Q3. Is there an EU-27 table of national primary energy factors (electricity, gas)?

### Takeaway
There is no single, current, official EU-27 table of the PEFs used in EPC methods. Annex I of the recast lets MS set their own forward-looking factors (reported via EN 17423) or use an EU-average electricity factor. Older compilations exist: a 2018 CA-EPBD factsheet and academic reviews.

### Cited Findings
- Annex I of 2024/1275:
  - Calculations must use "regularly updated and forward-looking primary energy factors" that separate non-renewable, renewable and total, or weighting factors per carrier.
  - Factors can be annual, seasonal, monthly, daily or hourly, and can be based on national, regional or local data.
  - MS report their choices and data sources according to EN 17423.
  - MS may use an average Union PEF for electricity instead of a national mix.

  — [EUR-Lex 2024/1275](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=OJ:L_202401275)
- The EU default electricity PEF stayed at 2.5 for years and was recalculated to 2.1 in the 2018 EED recast. Greece has used 2.9 since its 2010 EPBD transposition, not updated as of the paper. Poland has used 3.0 since a 2015 ordinance. Several MS use the EU default or do not update their national values. — [Balaras et al., "Primary Energy Factors for Electricity Production in Europe", Energies 16(1):93, 2023](https://www.mdpi.com/1996-1073/16/1/93) (open copy: [LAPSE](https://psecommunity.org/wp-content/plugins/wpor/includes/file/2302/LAPSE-2023.6391-1v1.pdf))
- Balaras et al. 2023 compute four PEF variants per EU-27 MS from Eurostat balances, with regressions for projection. Their Table 1 gives regressions, not the officially used regulatory values. — [same](https://psecommunity.org/wp-content/plugins/wpor/includes/file/2302/LAPSE-2023.6391-1v1.pdf)
- CA-EPBD 2018 factsheet, "Primary Energy Factors and Member States Energy Regulations" (2018, outdated). — [AAU portal entry](https://vbn.aau.dk/en/publications/primary-energy-factors-and-members-states-energy-regulations-prim/); listed file: [epbd-ca.eu PDF](http://epbd-ca.eu/wp-content/uploads/2018/04/05-CCT1-Factsheet-PEF.pdf)
- REHVA EPBD guidance (2024) on primary energy and operational CO2 indicator calculation in the revised EPBD. — [REHVA PDF](https://www.rehva.eu/fileadmin/user_upload/2024/EPBD_Guidance_2024.pdf)
- JRC NZEB report (2016): the primary energy indicator rests on non-homogeneous calculation methods across MS. — [JRC104344](https://publications.jrc.ec.europa.eu/repository/bitstream/JRC104344/development%20of%20the%20nzebs%20concept%20in%20member%20states_final.pdf)

### Inferences
- The app should store PEFs per country with a source and year for each value, and flag countries such as Greece (2.9) and Poland (3.0) where the regulatory values are known to be outdated relative to the grid mix.

### Gaps
- No verified EU-27 table of gas PEFs was found. Gas is typically about 1.0–1.1, but this was not confirmed from a source in this session.
- The 2018 CA factsheet values were not retrieved.
- Post-recast EN 17423 national reports are not yet compiled publicly, as far as found.

## Q4. Reference climates and EU-wide climate datasets

### Takeaway
Most MS set class boundaries without climate dependence. Climate enters through the calculation, the reference building, or a national reference climate. Only a few countries vary the boundaries themselves by climate.

### Cited Findings
- Climate in class boundaries (JRC 2023):
  - Italy used 6 climate zones in fixed boundaries until 2015 and then moved to a reference building.
  - Croatia has coastal and continental boundary sets.
  - France relaxes E/F/G boundaries for residential buildings in zones H1b, H1c and H2d above 800 m.
  - Most countries' boundaries are climate-independent.

  — [JRC135473](https://publications.jrc.ec.europa.eu/repository/bitstream/JRC135473/JRC135473_01.pdf)
- Other national climate handling (JRC 2023):
  - Austria calculates for the site climate and for an Austrian reference climate, and the EPC headline shows the reference climate.
  - Sweden uses 51 climatic (geographic adjustment) factors.
  - Croatia's heat-demand scale uses reference climate data.

  — [JRC135473](https://publications.jrc.ec.europa.eu/repository/bitstream/JRC135473/JRC135473_01.pdf)
- JRC notes that the reference-building approach means identical consumption can get different classes because of size or climate. — [JRC135473](https://publications.jrc.ec.europa.eu/repository/bitstream/JRC135473/JRC135473_01.pdf)

### Inferences
- For an app that re-rates one building under several national methods, the climate choice must be explicit: the national reference climate or the actual site. Austria and Croatia are the clearest cases of a mandated reference climate.

### Gaps
- EN ISO 52010-1 (climate data conversion), PVGIS TMY and JRC nZEB climate-zone datasets were not verified in this session. No sourced statements are made on them.

## Q5. Which countries are flagged as poorly documented, low-transparency or hard to compare?

### Takeaway
JRC 2023 could not collect register information for Belgium-Flanders, Latvia, Malta and Sweden. Malta and Poland had no class scale. The reference-building countries are structurally hard to compare on kWh. Greece, Hungary, Poland and Bulgaria are flagged by EPBD.wise as needing methodology, QC and rescaling work.

### Cited Findings
- EPC register data: publicly accessible in 9 MS, limited in 5, not accessible in 6, region-dependent in 3. Data missing for BE-Flanders, Latvia, Malta and Sweden. JRC states that "it is necessary to increase the number and quality of public information available" to judge EPC quality. — [JRC135473](https://publications.jrc.ec.europa.eu/repository/bitstream/JRC135473/JRC135473_01.pdf)
- Poland and Malta had no defined energy classes (as of 2023). — [JRC135473](https://publications.jrc.ec.europa.eu/repository/bitstream/JRC135473/JRC135473_01.pdf)
- EPBD.wise (2026) on BG, GR, HU and PL found common bottlenecks: insufficiently integrated databases, weak QA and capacity, non-actionable recommendations, low awareness. Two of the four prioritise simplifying the calculation methodology. — [EPBD.wise D5.3](https://www.bpie.eu/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/Energy-Performance-Certificates-v5-Compressed.pdf)
- PEFs not updated: Greece (2.9 since 2010) and Poland (3.0 since 2015). — [Balaras et al. 2023](https://psecommunity.org/wp-content/plugins/wpor/includes/file/2302/LAPSE-2023.6391-1v1.pdf)
- Good-practice (more transparent) examples cited:
  - Portugal: central database with more than 2.5 million EPCs and about 300 variables per building.
  - Denmark: owner-oriented EPC.
  - Ireland: QA and audit framework.
  - Sweden: expert training.

  — [EPBD.wise D5.3](https://www.bpie.eu/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/Energy-Performance-Certificates-v5-Compressed.pdf)

### Inferences
- Candidates for a "low transparency" flag in the app: Malta, Latvia, Poland (new scale), and possibly Bulgaria, Greece and Hungary. Each should be confirmed by the per-country researchers.

### Gaps
- No single source ranks all 27 on methodological transparency.

## Q6. Studies that rated the same building under multiple national EPC methods (the label gap)

### Takeaway
Jenkins, Sayfikar, Gomez & Fueyo (Buildings, 2024) is the key study. They ran sample buildings through several national EPC methods. About 38% of buildings got the same class, 45% differed by one class, and the extreme case differed by four classes (C vs G). The differences came from how gains, ventilation and reference values are treated.

### Cited Findings
- Jenkins D.P., Sayfikar M., Gomez A., Fueyo N. (2024), "A Comparative Study of Energy Performance Certificates across Europe", Buildings 14(9):2906, doi:10.3390/buildings14092906.
  - They propose a process for comparing EPCs of the same building across countries.
  - Similar climates often give similar-looking ratings that "hide real differences" in method.
  - EU-level EPC reform needs a bottom-up understanding of the national schemes.

  — [Heriot-Watt portal](https://researchportal.hw.ac.uk/en/publications/a-comparative-study-of-energy-performance-certificates-across-eur/); [MDPI](https://www.mdpi.com/2075-5309/14/9/2906)
- Same study, from a search summary (the MDPI page returned 403, so this was not read in full):
  - 38% of sampled buildings got the same class under different national methods, and 45% differed by one class.
  - A Slovenian educational building was C under the Croatian method and G under the Slovenian one, attributed mainly to internal and solar gains and ventilation-loss calculations.
  - Reference-building (proportional) scales make ratings depend on the reference used.

  — [MDPI Buildings 14(9):2906](https://www.mdpi.com/2075-5309/14/9/2906) (medium confidence; verify the numbers in the full text)
- Follow-up: Sayfikar & Jenkins (2025), on EPC calculation methods across Europe and new indicators (BSER&T). — [SAGE](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/01436244241282076)
- JRC 2023 makes the conceptual point: with the reference-building method, buildings with identical consumption can receive different classes, and fixed boundaries make like-for-like comparison easier. — [JRC135473](https://publications.jrc.ec.europa.eu/repository/bitstream/JRC135473/JRC135473_01.pdf)
- Related, on the certified-versus-real gap rather than the cross-country gap: "Energy Efficiency in Buildings: The Gap Between Energy Certification Methods and Real Performances", Energies 2025. — [doi:10.3390/en18226015](https://doi.org/10.3390/en18226015)

### Inferences
- The app's core idea (one building, many national labels) has a direct peer-reviewed precedent, Jenkins et al. 2024. A typical spread of 0–1 classes, with outliers up to 4, is a defensible headline if confirmed in the full text.

### Gaps
- Which countries Jenkins et al. compared, and how many buildings, could not be confirmed (403). From the abstract and snippet, they include at least Slovenia, Croatia, Spain and the UK, which matches the authors' affiliations (Heriot-Watt and Zaragoza); this is unverified.
- No other multi-country same-building study was verified in this session.
